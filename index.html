Four gym sessions a week the way you want them: two leg days, one pull day, one push day, plus Pilates on Thursday. Sessions 45–60 minutes. The whole game is progressive overload: beat last week, even by a rep.

How to progress. Pick a weight you can do for the bottom of the rep range with good form. Each week add a rep or two. When you hit the top of the range on every set, go up a little in weight and start again. Log the main lifts on the Track tab. Rest 60–90s between sets, up to 2 min on the big lifts. Warm up 5 min plus a lighter set before your first working set.

Pilates — Thursday night

Keep it, it's a brilliant complement to the lifting. It builds core strength and hip stability (which makes your squats, hip thrusts and RDLs stronger and safer), improves posture and mobility, and works as gentle active recovery between gym days. No special fuelling, just a normal day of eating. Think of it as part of the training, not extra to it.

Running — how to build from 3k

You can do 3k comfortably, so start there and build slowly. The aim is a comfortable 5k, then you can play with pace. Keep every run easy and conversational, running is the supporting act here, not the main event, so it shouldn't leave you wrecked for the gym.

  • Weeks 1–2: 3k easy, once or twice a week. Nail the easy pace, you should be able to hold a conversation.
  • Weeks 3–4: push to 3.5–4k. Add distance or pace, never both at once.
  • Weeks 5–6: build to 4.5–5k. A local parkrun is a nice way to hit 5k with company.
  • Once 5k feels easy: keep one easy 5k, and if you want, add the odd faster run or a few intervals.

Rules of thumb: don't run the day after a leg day if your legs are heavy, and if running ever starts eating into your gym recovery, cut it back. The lifting comes first for the shape you're after.

Eat a bit more than feels natural. To build, you need a small surplus, a little more food than you'd naturally reach for. On a slim frame that's the number one lever. You don't do it with giant meals, you do it by never skipping a feed and adding calorie-dense extras: a scoop of protein, cheese, olive oil on the veg, a handful of nuts. If your weight holds dead still for a few weeks, add a feed.

A gym morning, before school

The order that works: 1. Small carb snack when you wake (see below), 20–30 min before you train. 2. Train. 3. Protein shake straight after (keep one in your bag). 4. Your reheatable breakfast at school mid-morning, protein and carbs to properly refuel. Then your normal feeds through the day. On Pilates and rest days you don't need the early snack, just eat normally with protein at each feed.

Pre-gym carb snack (small & early)

Something small and easy on the stomach, 20–30 minutes before: a Pink Lady apple, a couple of rice cakes with honey, a small pot of oats, or a slice of toast with honey or jam. If you really can't eat that early, even half an apple or a few sips of a carb/electrolyte drink will do, then get your protein in straight after.

Breakfasts you can heat up at school

Made ahead, reheated in the staff-room microwave. These are your gym-day breakfasts. Tap any for the recipe.

Picky plates & snacks

When a full meal feels like too much, build a picky plate: a protein, a carb, something fresh. It feels like snacking but does a real job.

Weekend drinks

Factored in, not banned. A night out is fine. Alcohol just blunts muscle-building and recovery for about a day and adds empty calories, so: eat a proper protein-rich meal before, alternate drinks with water, and get protein and real food in the next day rather than writing it off. The biggest setback isn't the drinks, it's the hungover day where you skip the gym and barely eat.
Quick notes. No Greek yoghurt, no peanut butter, no beans. Protein comes from meat, fish, eggs, milk, cheese, cottage cheese, protein powder and the pancakes. Fruit is Pink Lady apples, so lean on oats, rice, potatoes, pasta, bread and crackers for carbs. Keep a shake and a couple of picky bits in the fridge so there's always an easy feed.

What actually changes your shape

You're naturally lean, which is a good start, but a lean frame doesn't change on its own. What changes it is building muscle, and muscle needs a training signal and enough food to build with. The signal is progressive overload. The food is a small surplus with plenty of protein. For someone slim who under-eats without realising, the food side is almost always the missing piece.

Why frequent small feeds

You can't comfortably finish big plates, and forcing them makes eating a chore you'll quit. Five or six smaller feeds hit the same daily total without the dread, keep protein topped up for building, and fit a teacher's day. Your grazing habit isn't a problem to fix, it's the method.

Protein around 2g per kg is the sweet spot for building, spread across the day. The surplus only needs to be small, a couple of hundred calories over what you burn. Pilates supports the lifting, and running stays easy so it doesn't eat into the build.

The scale is not the scoreboard

Don't judge this by the scales. The real signals are your lifts going up on the Track tab, your clothes fitting differently, and how you look over a couple of months. You may gain a kilo or two, and if your lifts are climbing, that's muscle doing its job. Weigh monthly at most, if at all.

Alcohol, honestly

A weekend drink is part of life and it's built in. Alcohol lowers the muscle-building response for around a day after training and adds calories that don't feed the goal, so a good meal first, water alongside, and a protein-rich day after. Enjoy it, then get back to it.
