Set your week

Tap to add or remove. Leave a day empty for rest. It opens on your usual shape — change whatever's different.

A four-day Upper/Lower split, each muscle trained twice a week, which is what builds shape efficiently around a busy job. Sessions run 45–60 minutes. The whole game is progressive overload: beat last week, even by a rep.

How to progress (this is the important bit). Pick a weight you can do for the bottom of the rep range with good form. Each week, add a rep or two. Once you hit the top of the range on every set, go up a little in weight next time and start again. Log the main lifts on the Track tab, if the numbers climb, the muscle follows. Rest 60–90s between sets, up to 2 min on the heavy compound lifts. Warm up 5 min plus a lighter set or two before your first big lift.

The two runs

Easy run

30–40 min at a conversational pace (Zone 2). Builds your aerobic base and helps recovery without burning the fuel you need to build. Ideally not straight after a heavy leg day.

Faster run

Once a week. A parkrun 5k, or 20–30 min with intervals (e.g. 6–8 × 1 min hard, 1–2 min easy). Keep it to one, cardio is a supporting act here, not the main event.

Eat a bit more than feels natural. To build, you need to be in a small surplus, a little more food than you'd naturally reach for. On a slim frame that's the number one lever. You don't do it with giant meals, you do it by never skipping a feed and adding calorie-dense extras: a handful of oats, cheese, olive oil on the veg, a shake. If your weight holds dead still for a few weeks, you're not eating enough, add a feed.

The picky plate — your secret weapon

When a full meal feels like too much, build a picky plate instead. The rule: a protein, a carb, and something fresh. Aim for a proper hit of protein in it. Examples: cold chicken or ham + cheese + crackers + veg sticks + hummus + a Pink Lady apple. Boiled eggs + cheese + oatcakes. Tuna or salmon + rice cakes + cucumber. It feels like snacking, but it's a real meal doing a real job.

Before

You train after school, so don't go in empty. 60–90 min before: some carbs plus a bit of protein. A slice of toast with peanut butter, oatcakes and cheese, or a small bowl of porridge. Nothing heavy.

After

Within an hour: protein plus carbs. A shake with milk and a banana or oats, or go straight to the protein pancakes (see Treats), then dinner not long after. This window matters most on lifting days.

Weekend drinks

Factored in, not banned. A night out is fine. Just know alcohol blunts muscle-building and recovery for about a day and adds empty calories, so: eat a proper protein-rich meal before you go, alternate drinks with water, and get protein and real food in the next day rather than writing it off. The biggest setback isn't the drinks, it's the hungover day where you skip the gym and barely eat. Don't let one night cost you two days.

The sweet stuff, made to earn its place. These give you a proper hit of protein while scratching the sweet itch, so use them strategically: post-workout when your body wants protein and carbs, or as an evening treat that quietly tops up your day. Tap any for how to make it.

The base protein pancakes. Blend 40g oats, 1 scoop (30g) vanilla or chocolate protein, 1 egg, a splash of milk and a teaspoon of baking powder into a batter. Cook small pancakes in a non-stick pan with a little spray oil, 1–2 min a side. Makes a stack with ~30–35g protein. That's the canvas, the toppings below make it different every time.

Other strategic sweets

How to use them. On a lifting day, have the pancakes (or a protein mug cake) as your post-workout feed, protein and carbs when they count most. On a rest day, if you want something sweet, reach for one of these rather than something that gives you nothing back. That's the whole idea: the treat does a job.

What actually changes your shape

You're naturally lean, which is a good starting point, but a lean frame doesn't change on its own. What changes it is building muscle, and muscle needs two things: a training signal and enough food to build with. The training signal is progressive overload, lifting a bit more over time. The food is a small calorie surplus with plenty of protein. Miss either and nothing much happens. For someone slim who under-eats without realising, the food side is almost always the missing piece.

Why frequent small feeds, not big meals

You can't comfortably finish large plates, and forcing them just makes eating a chore you'll quit. Five or six smaller feeds hits the same daily total without the dread, keeps protein topped up through the day for muscle building, and fits a teacher's day where you can't sit down to a big lunch anyway. Your grazing habit isn't a problem to fix, it's the method.

Protein at roughly 2g per kg is the sweet spot for building muscle, spread across the day. The surplus only needs to be small, a couple of hundred calories over what you burn; more than that just adds fat, not muscle. Runs stay easy so they don't eat into it.

The scale is not the scoreboard

Don't judge this by the scales. The real signals are your lifts going up on the Track tab, your clothes fitting differently, and how you look in the mirror over a couple of months. You may gain a kilo or two, and if your lifts are climbing, that's muscle doing exactly what you asked it to. Weigh monthly at most, if at all.

Alcohol, honestly

A weekend drink is part of life and it's built in. The straight facts: alcohol lowers the muscle-building response for around a day after training and adds calories that don't feed the goal. So it's not off-limits, it just pays to be smart, a good meal first, water alongside, and a proper protein-rich day after. Enjoy it, then get back to it.
